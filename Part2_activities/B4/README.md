## B4 - Participate in 3 in-class activities in labs (facilitators will administer such activities)
### Description: 
I took part in three cybersecurity-related in-class lab exercises that the facilitators organised. These exercises emphasised teamwork, observation, practical cybersecurity ideas, and using defensive security thinking in real-world situations.
### Activities:
1. **Lab 2: Privacy**
In the Lab 2 privacy exercise, students were instructed to come up with a three or four-digit number and provide other students hints without really disclosing the number. The class then determined the average findings after other students tried to figure out the secret number using partial information. This exercise emphasised the value of privacy and restricting unwanted information sharing while illustrating how seemingly little details might eventually expose critical information.
2. **Lab 5: IDS**
Students evaluated several physical and cybersecurity-related safety aspects as they walked around the university campus during this in-class exercise. To find and assess security controls such door lock mechanisms, access control for specialised labs, monitoring systems, emergency evacuation information, Wi-Fi connectivity, and potential security flaws, we used a worksheet. Students were prompted to consider cybersecurity and physical security threats in real-world settings. It also illustrated the need for organisations to take into account several security levels, such as network dependability, access control, surveillance, and emergency readiness.
3. **Lab 3: Access Control**
Our group observed and noted details about the workplace and surroundings of another group during a reconnaissance-style activity in the classroom. This includes noticeable items and features like pencils on laptops and blue pencil boxes next to laptops. Through observation and reconnaissance methods, attackers can obtain valuable information without physically engaging with systems, as the action illustrated.
### Analysis:
The practical cybersecurity concepts of privacy, reconnaissance, physical security, and environmental risk assessment were reinforced by these in-class exercises. The exercises showed how information collecting and possible security threats might be influenced by minute details of revealed or observable information. Additionally, they emphasised the significance of monitoring, access control, defensive thinking, and safeguarding both digital and physical surroundings in cybersecurity.
